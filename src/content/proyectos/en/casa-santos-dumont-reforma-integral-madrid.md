## A dialogue between Madrid and Rio

Designed for a young, connected and cosmopolitan client, the project sets up a dialogue between Madrid and Rio de Janeiro. The architecture opens up to daylight and to the outdoors — the treetops of Madrid Río become part of the everyday view — with a natural colour palette. The home becomes a social space where music, light and life flow naturally.

## Curves and function

The project is named after the Brazilian aviator Alberto Santos-Dumont, a symbol of innovation and lightness. His motto — "the important thing is not to fly, but to know how to land" — translates into architecture where every decision balances aesthetics and function.

## An optimised 87 m² layout

- Three fully daylit façades
- Floor-to-ceiling windows
- The corridor removed to gain usable space
- An adaptable open/closed kitchen
- A second bathroom added
- The social area as the heart of the home

The private area has three balanced bedrooms, one with a floor-to-ceiling balcony, built-in wardrobes throughout and a separate utility room.

## A distinctive interior

- Base palette: indigo, chocolate and gold, with red and orange accents
- Curved furniture in harmony with the architecture
- Marazzi porcelain tiles in the kitchen and main bedroom
- Geometric mosaic floors
- Bathrooms inspired by Carrara marble
