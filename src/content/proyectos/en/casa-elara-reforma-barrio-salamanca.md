## When transforming means understanding

The starting point was familiar: a classic Salamanca-district apartment built in 1930, with a layout that no longer matched the way we live today — fragmented rooms, pointless circulation, wasted daylight and no connection between spaces.

This renovation does not respond with excess. It responds with precision: not adding layers, but revealing the potential of the space by removing everything that was not needed.

## Light as a tool

Before the works, daylight was there but played no part. Now it runs through the home without obstacles, ordering and defining it. Artificial lighting accompanies it rather than competing with it: integrated general lighting, lines of indirect light and precise accent points.

## Coherence before impact

The interior design works as a system. Materials were chosen for coherence and durability, not display:

- High-resistance sintered stone
- Large-format marble-effect cladding
- Natural oak
- Integrated bespoke joinery

The palette stays restrained: whites, greys, wood and touches of brass.

## 138 m² designed around everyday life

The home is organised into three en-suite bedrooms, a main open space where kitchen and living room share the light, a guest toilet and a concealed utility room. What matters is not the layout itself but the relationship between spaces: nothing is isolated.

## Comfort you feel rather than see

Thermally and acoustically insulated windows, solar-control glazing, efficient ventilation and integrated home automation improve daily life without imposing themselves. Technology is perceived in the silence, the temperature and the light.
